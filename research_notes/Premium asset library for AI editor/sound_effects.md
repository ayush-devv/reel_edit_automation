# Sound-effects (SFX) sources for an autonomous AI reel editor (client work)

Research date: 2026-11-19. All pages accessed 2026-11-19 unless noted. "Verbatim" quotes were obtained through a page-reading tool that caps quotes at ~125 characters, so long clauses are reproduced as consecutive fragments. Context: the engine already holds 252 SFX (47 synthesised + 205 Kenney CC0), skewed to hits/clicks. Gaps: whooshes, risers, booms, transitions, ambience, crowd/laughs, UI/notification, cash/typing.

Pipeline facts the licences are judged against: (a) paying freelance clients; (b) finished vertical reels published on Instagram / YouTube Shorts / TikTok; (c) an AI agent (Claude) *selects and places* SFX from a locally indexed library (no model training on the audio); (d) raw SFX files are never delivered to clients.

---

## 1. Licence for commercial client work on social platforms (attribution, NC/SA/ND traps, redistribution of raw files)

### Takeaway
Nearly every source allows finished-video commercial/client use and bans redistributing the raw files. The traps are: BBC RemArc (non-commercial only), Freesound CC BY-NC and legacy Sampling+ sounds, Zapsplat free tier (attribution required), ElevenLabs free tier (non-commercial), and Artlist Social / Epidemic Personal tiers (no client work).

### Cited Findings
**Free / open sources**
- **Kenney**: assets are CC0. Kenney's support page says "You're free to use them, even in commercial projects" and "Attribution is not required." It asks users not to use the Kenney logo separately. — [Kenney support](https://kenney.nl/support)
- **Pixabay** (Content License covers "royalty-free images, video, audio and other media"): free to use, no attribution, may modify. The licence says "You cannot sell or distribute Content (either in digital or physical form) on a Standalone basis." It defines Standalone as "where no creative effort has been applied to the Content and it remains in substantially the same form as it exists on the Service." — [Pixabay licence summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/); [Pixabay Terms, last updated 20 Oct 2026](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)
- Pixabay says "If Content contains any recognisable trademarks, logos or brands, you cannot use that Content for commercial purposes." The summary has no SFX-specific terms. — [Pixabay licence summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/)
- **Freesound**: each sound carries its own licence. Current options are CC0, CC BY and CC BY-NC. Older uploads stay under CC Sampling+ unless re-licensed. Run by MTG at Universitat Pompeu Fabra (UPF). — [Wikipedia: Freesound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freesound)
- CC0 needs no attribution. CC BY allows commercial use with author credit. CC BY-NC requires credit and is non-commercial. — [Freesound forum via search summary](https://freesound.org/forum/legal-help-and-attribution-questions/43120/?page=1); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freesound)
- **BBC Sound Effects (RemArc licence)**: the free archive (~33,000 files reported) is for personal, educational and research use only. Commercial use needs a paid licence through Pro Sound Effects. The archive's download notice excludes commercial use and points to PSE via each recording's "Show details" panel. — [Swarmify review](https://swarmify.com/blog/free-sound-effects-websites/); [DIYPhotography](https://www.diyphotography.net/the-bbc-sound-effects-archive-is-now-free-to-download-with-over-33000-files/); [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/bbc-sound-effect-archive-free-audio-samples-news)
- A Freesound forum post quotes the RemArc commercial test: if you "monetise it, sell it, or charge for access to it", or it is "advertising-funded or commercially sponsored", it counts as commercial. — [Freesound forum](https://freesound.org/forum/legal-help-and-attribution-questions/45079)
- **Mixkit SFX**: secondary sources say Mixkit SFX may be used in personal and commercial projects without attribution. One university guide lists attribution as "No, but appreciated". — [Newcastle Uni PDF](https://www.newcastle.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/875995/Suggested-sound-effects-sites.pdf); [Wyzowl](https://wyzowl.com/free-sound-effects-sites/)
- The official Mixkit licence page (mixkit.co/license) renders its licence text with JavaScript, so it could not be retrieved verbatim (see Gaps). — [Mixkit licence page](https://mixkit.co/license/)
- **99Sounds**: "All audio files and plugins available for download via 99Sounds are royalty-free for personal and commercial projects." Credit "is not obligatory." The licence also says "You do not have the right to sell or redistribute (even for free) the audio files", and bars use in noise, white-noise and sleep-sound apps. — [99Sounds licence](https://99sounds.org/license/)
- **Sonniss GDC bundles (licence v2.0, effective 27 Aug 2026)**: headed "UNLIMITED USER LICENSE". It allows use and modification "for personal and commercial projects" on "an unlimited number of projects", "without attribution to the original creator". — [Sonniss GDC licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- Sonniss restricts raw files: "Licensee may not distribute, publish, sub-license or otherwise supply the sound effects as sound effects" to others. Finished work is fine: "This does not restrict the Licensee's finished projects, which may be shared, published and sold in the normal way." — [Sonniss GDC licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- Sonniss allows sharing the sounds with "people in the Licensee's own team or organisation who are working on the Licensee's projects." The words clients, contractors and freelancers do not appear. — [Sonniss GDC licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- The Sonniss bundle page says the sounds are "licensed for media production (games, film, TV, interactive projects) only", "completely royalty-free for any commercial work", and covered by a non-expiring lifetime licence. — [Sonniss GDC page](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)
- **Adobe Audition / Creative Cloud bundled SFX**: Adobe calls them "thousands of uncompressed, royalty-free audio sound effects" governed by the Adobe Software License Agreement. Community answers (not official) read the EULA as allowing any project, with resale of the raw effects as the main restriction. — [Adobe Audition SFX downloads](https://www.adobe.com/products/audition/offers/adobeauditiondlcsfx.html); [Adobe Community thread](https://community.adobe.com/t5/audition-discussions/can-i-use-these-sound-effects-for-free/m-p/9145770/highlight/true)

**Freemium / paid**
- **Zapsplat Standard licence (last updated 8 May 2026)**:
  - Free (Basic) users: "Attribution Required: You must provide clear credit to "ZapSplat" wherever reasonably possible in your project."
  - Free users may use sounds in "an unlimited number of personal, commercial, and broadcast projects worldwide", including "YouTube videos, Twitch streams, and other online media" and social media.
  - Client work (Section 6) is allowed if the audio is "incorporated into a larger end product and is not distributed as standalone audio files."
  - Redistribution is prohibited "under any circumstances".
  - Premium removes attribution, adds WAV, and unlimited downloads under a Fair Use Policy. Premium sounds downloaded while active stay licensed attribution-free for life.
  - Source: [Zapsplat Standard licence](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/)
- **ElevenLabs Sound Effects (terms last updated 9 Oct 2026)**:
  - Output may be used "for commercial purposes only where your plan permits commercial use".
  - "you may not sell, resell, license, sublicense, distribute, or otherwise commercially exploit Output … as isolated audio files or samples."
  - The free plan carries no commercial licence. Paid plans (from Starter) include it.
  - Sources: [ElevenLabs SFX terms](https://elevenlabs.io/sound-effects-terms); [ElevenLabs help: publishing generated content](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/can-i-publish-the-content-i-generate-on-the-platform/llms.txt); [pricing round-up](https://bigvu.tv/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-2026-plans-credits-commercial-rights-api-costs/)
- **Envato (Elements) licence (last revised 6 Oct 2026)**:
  - Clause 10a.i: "You can use an Item to create an End Product for yourself or for a client of yours." The licence must be sublicensed to the client, limited to the End Product.
  - Clause 12b bars redistributing an Item "as stock, in a tool or template, or with source files".
  - Clause 12e: "You must not permit an end user to extract an Item and use it separately."
  - Sound effects are not singled out. Music has extra rules (clauses 14–15).
  - Source: [Envato licence terms](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-License-Terms)
- **Artlist**: client work, paid/promoted video and broadcast need the Pro plan or higher. Social covers 1 channel per platform, own channels only, no client work. The client gets the finished project, not your source files. Clearlist links let a client's YouTube channel be cleared (30-day link). This is from a third-party guide (verified 2026-11-07) summarising Artlist's licence effective 15 Feb 2026. — [CC Hound Artlist licence guide](https://www.cchound.com/artlist/artlist-license/)
- **Epidemic Sound**: the Pro plan lists "Freelancer client work licensing". A third-party comparison says the Personal plan is not for business or client work. — [Epidemic Pro plan](https://www.epidemicsound.com/our-plans/pro-plan/); [LicenseOrg comparison](https://licenseorg.com/compare/epidemic-sound-vs-artlist)
- **Soundsnap**: the Pro tier is "designed for broad commercial uses including: social media content" and covers client work and monetisation. Pro+ adds broadcast, OTT, out-of-home advertising, and apps, films or games over US$500k. Redistributing the Sounds "as they are" in any context is prohibited. — [Soundsnap licence](https://www.soundsnap.com/licence)
- **A Sound Effect (marketplace EULA, dateModified 2026-10-17)**: grants a "worldwide, non-exclusive, perpetual, royalty-free license(s)". Use is limited to "as many of the Licensee's workstations at a time as the number of licenses" bought. Distribution is allowed only where sounds are "incorporated in and synchronized with other media productions". The EULA applies to all libraries "unless explicitly stated", so individual libraries may carry their own terms. — [A Sound Effect licence agreement](https://asoundeffect.com/license-agreement)
- **Motion Array**: projects completed and published during an active subscription stay covered. Cancelled subscribers can't use assets in new or unpublished projects. — [Motion Array licence FAQ](https://help.motionarray.com/hc/en-us/articles/37251636136605-License-FAQs)
- **Storyblocks Individual License (28 Sep 2026)**:
  - §1.3 covers "commercial work you perform directly for your own clients". But it is not for "reliance on this license by any entity, company, or client"; organisations or multiple people need another licence.
  - §2.1 bars making files available separately, including "sharing raw files with clients and/or colleagues".
  - §2.6: "You may not use any Stock File to compete with Storyblocks or create a library of content for distribution."
  - §3: audio files may not be uploaded or shared "with any digital streaming platform" on their own.
  - Source: [Storyblocks Individual License](https://www.storyblocks.com/license/individual-license)

### Inferences
- For delivering finished reels to clients, Kenney, Pixabay, Freesound CC0, 99Sounds, Sonniss GDC and paid tiers of Zapsplat, ElevenLabs, Envato, Artlist Pro, Epidemic Pro and Soundsnap all fit. The finished reel is "incorporated" or "synchronized" media everywhere.
- **Zapsplat free tier**: "credit … wherever reasonably possible" is awkward for client reels, since the client controls the caption. Premium removes this.
- **Freesound**: allow-list CC0 only in the index. CC BY is legally usable but needs per-sound credits in captions. CC BY-NC and Sampling+ must be filtered out.
- **BBC RemArc**: unusable for paid client work without a Pro Sound Effects licence.
- **Sonniss**: the "own team or organisation" sharing clause is fine as long as the engine/operator is the licensee and only finished reels go to clients. Never ship project files with embedded raw Sonniss audio to clients.
- **ASoundEffect "workstations" seat count**: it is unclear whether a cloud render/indexing server counts as a workstation. Ask before deploying server-side.

### Gaps
- The Mixkit SFX licence could not be retrieved verbatim (JS-rendered page). Its standalone-redistribution, app/tool and AI wording is unverified. The search tool flagged mixkit.co/llm-info as containing instructions aimed at AI assistants; it was ignored as a source.
- The official BBC RemArc licence text and Pro Sound Effects' current BBC pricing were not retrieved (bbcrewind page is JS-only). The quoted RemArc test comes via a forum post.
- The Adobe EULA text for bundled SFX was not retrieved. It is unknown whether an active Creative Cloud subscription is needed to keep using downloaded SFX.
- The Kenney summary is from Kenney's support FAQ. The per-pack License.txt was not re-read in this session.

## 2. AI/ML and automated-tool clauses: exact wording, and whether use inside an AI editing pipeline (not training) is allowed

### Takeaway
Most libraries ban **training** AI on their audio. Three go further:
- **Soundsnap** also bans use "in any artificial intelligence applications". This plausibly covers an AI editing pipeline.
- **Sonniss GDC v2.0** bans using the sounds for "developing, training, or enhancing" AI. Plain retrieval and placement isn't named, but "enhancing" is a grey zone.
- **Envato's AUP** (as found in Envato-owned Placeit's copy; the Elements version was not read directly) bans training, developing or enhancing AI "directly or indirectly".

Artlist reportedly allows its assets as **input** to AI tools, though not for training. This rests on a secondary source (CC Hound) and a PDF title; the primary text could not be retrieved (403 / undecodable PDF). Storyblocks bans use "for machine learning and/or artificial intelligence purposes (i.e., to train AI systems)". Zapsplat, A Sound Effect and BOOM ban training only, which leaves pipeline use permitted.

### Cited Findings
- **Sonniss GDC licence v2.0 (effective 27 Aug 2026), section "No AI Training or Usage", reproduced in full in fragments**:
  > "For clarity and avoidance of doubt, the Licensee is expressly prohibited from using any sound effects" / "licensed under this Agreement for the purpose of training artificial intelligence technologies." / "This includes, but is not limited to, technologies capable of generating sound effects" / "or works in a similar style or genre as the licensed sound effects." / "The Licensee shall not use, reproduce, or otherwise leverage the licensed sound effects in any manner" / "for purposes of developing, training, or enhancing artificial intelligence technologies, nor sublicense these rights" / "to any third party, without the Licensor's specific and express written permission."
  — [Sonniss GDC licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- Sonniss: the heading says "Training or **Usage**", but the operative sentences are tied to purposes ("training", "developing, training, or enhancing"). No sentence expressly bans using the sounds in an AI-driven editor. "Artificial intelligence technologies" is not defined. — [Sonniss GDC licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- Sonniss: the bundle page summarises the licence as "Use for AI/ML training is strictly prohibited under our license terms." — [Sonniss GDC page](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)
- Sonniss version rule: v2.0 governs downloads on or after 27 Aug 2026. Earlier downloads are governed by the version published on the day of download. — [Sonniss GDC licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- **Soundsnap, Section 3 (restrictions)**:
  > "Use the Sounds and any associated metadata or information in, or related to, (A) training machine learning models," / "whether generative or non-generative, and/or (B) in any artificial intelligence applications." / "To utilize any Sounds for such purposes, a special license or explicit permission is required."
  — [Soundsnap licence](https://www.soundsnap.com/licence)
- **Zapsplat (8 May 2026)**: Section 5 prohibits using sounds "to train Artificial Intelligence (AI) or machine learning models of any kind." Section 8: "Automated access (bots, scrapers, scripts) is prohibited." — [Zapsplat Standard licence](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/)
- **A Sound Effect EULA**: its restriction covers usage "for the purpose of training, improving, or developing any Neural Networks (NN)" / "Artificial Intelligence (AI) or Machine Learning (ML) models or algorithms, whether for commercial or non-commercial use." — [A Sound Effect licence](https://asoundeffect.com/license-agreement)
- **BOOM Library**: its Terms & Conditions list as a prohibited use "AI/ML Training: Developing or training Neural Networks, Artificial Intelligence, or Machine Learning models". — [BOOM Library T&C (via search)](https://www.boomlibrary.com/?p=480427)
- BOOM's PlugIn EULA separately lists restricted uses including "audio data processing, audio data training". It may be an older document. — [BOOM PlugIn EULA PDF](https://www.boomlibrary.com/metadata/00_BOOM_PlugIn_EULA.pdf)
- **Sound Ideas** (which hosts a BOOM terms PDF): "The Licensee agrees that the Licensed Material shall not be used, in whole or in part, as a basis for the creation or training of artificial intelligence (AI) models, machine learning algorithms, or any similar computational systems." — [Sound Ideas / BOOM terms PDF](https://www.sound-ideas.com/pdf/Terms%20of%20Services%20Boom%20Library.pdf)
- **Pixabay Terms (20 Oct 2026), Section 8**: use of the Service "is strictly prohibited for all unauthorised purposes, including without limitation for machine learning purposes." — [Pixabay Terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)
- **Motion Array ToS**: bars use of "any Motion Array Products and/or assets and/or data and/or information and/or Motion Array site for purposes of machine learning and/or Artificial Intelligence training", and generative AI creation. — [Motion Array ToS](https://adobe-panel-api.motionarray.com/terms-of-service)
- **Envato**: the Elements licence (6 Oct 2026) has no AI clause; it links a separate "Envato AI Product Terms". — [Envato licence terms](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-License-Terms)
- Envato's Acceptable Use Policy (Placeit copy, rev. Sept 2025) makes "the use of Envato Sites or Assets to train, develop, or enhance artificial intelligence models or machine learning algorithms, whether directly or indirectly" unauthorised. — [Envato/Placeit AUP](https://help.placeit.net/hc/en-us/articles/43602734826137-Acceptable-Use-Policy)
- **Artlist**: its Acceptable Use Policy bars using any Asset or Output as part of a dataset or to train or support AI systems. — [Artlist AUP](https://artlist.io/help-center/privacy-terms/acceptable-use-policy/)
- Artlist hosts a licence PDF titled "current pro license permitting assets in AI" (dated 01-04-2026). Its body could not be decoded here. — [Artlist Pro licence PDF](https://help-center.artlist.io/media/w5mglb2w/current-pro-license-permitting-assets-in-ai-clean-01042026.pdf)
- A third-party guide summarises Artlist's current rules as: assets may be used "as input, but not to train models". It also bars sending assets to services that claim rights over outputs or show them to other users. AI-edited assets "still count as an Asset." — [CC Hound](https://www.cchound.com/artlist/artlist-license/)
- **Epidemic Sound**: a third-party reproduction of its end-user terms lists training ML/AI systems among prohibited uses of its music. The official SFX wording was not found. — [elevate.io copy of Epidemic terms](https://elevate.io/legal/epidemic-sound)
- **Storyblocks Individual License (last updated 28 Sep 2026), §2.9**: "You may not use any Stock File (including any caption information, keywords or other metadata associated with content)" / "for machine learning and/or artificial intelligence purposes (i.e., to train AI systems)".
  - The clause also contains a carve-out fragment: "in the creation of projects that do not transform the underlying Stock Files". Its exact scope could not be read in context.
  - Storyblocks also sells a separate "data licensing" product that clears its SFX for ML and generative training.
  - Sources: [Storyblocks Individual License](https://www.storyblocks.com/license/individual-license); [Storyblocks data licensing](https://www.storyblocks.com/business-solutions/data-licensing)
- **Soundly**: its Freesound add-on has "mixed licenses for commercial use". Soundly advises choosing "Creative Commons 0 only" for commercial work. Its FAQ page has no AI clause. — [Soundly FAQ](https://getsoundly.com/faq/how-can-i-use-the-freesound-library)
- **Stability AI Community License** (governs Stability models): free commercial use below "$1M (or local currency equivalent) of annual revenue". Per the FAQ, "you own outputs generated from the Core Models or Derivative Works". Outputs may not be used to create a new model architecture. The page lists "Stable Audio 3.0" but does not name Stable Audio Open 1.0, which the model card says is under the "Stability AI Community License". — [Stability AI licence](https://stability.ai/license); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-audio-open-1.0)
- **Freesound API terms**: no AI/ML training clause. — [Freesound API ToS](https://freesound.org/help/tos_api/)
- **99Sounds**: no AI clause. — [99Sounds licence](https://99sounds.org/license/)
- **Stable Audio Open 1.0**: trained on about 486,492 recordings, all CC0, CC BY or CC Sampling+. That is 472,618 from Freesound and 13,874 from the Free Music Archive. — [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-audio-open-1.0)

### Inferences
- **"Use inside an AI editing pipeline" is lowest-risk** at Kenney (CC0), Freesound CC0, 99Sounds, Zapsplat, A Sound Effect, BOOM and Artlist (explicit input permission). Their AI clauses only target training or developing models.
- **Soundsnap's "(B) in any artificial intelligence applications" is the clearest bar.** An autonomous Claude editor that picks and places Soundsnap sounds is plausibly an "AI application". Exclude Soundsnap unless it grants a special licence in writing.
- **Sonniss (risk depends on how the engine handles the sounds).** Plain keyword/metadata indexing plus Claude choosing sounds by name is not "training". It arguably isn't "developing … or enhancing" AI either, because the AI is unchanged.
  - Grey zone: computing audio embeddings (e.g. CLAP) over Sonniss files, or logging which sounds worked to tune selection, could be read as "leverag[ing] … for purposes of … enhancing artificial intelligence technologies".
  - The heading "No AI Training or Usage" invites the broader reading.
  - Recommended mitigation: email Sonniss for "specific and express written permission" describing the exact use (retrieval/placement only, no training, no embeddings, no redistribution). Keep the reply on file.
  - Also record the download date of each bundle, because pre-27-Aug-2026 downloads fall under the earlier licence version.
- **Pixabay's "machine learning purposes" ban** applies to use of the Service, not explicitly to downloaded Content in a product. Automated, AI-driven selection from Pixabay is still a grey area, and scraping is explicitly banned (see Q3).
- **Envato's "directly or indirectly … enhance"** wording is broad enough to be uncomfortable for an AI product built on Envato assets. Treat it as medium risk.

### Gaps
- The pre-v2.0 Sonniss GDC licence text was not retrieved. It matters if bundles were downloaded before 27 Aug 2026, as the owner decided on 18 Nov 2026 to use Sonniss (see project memory). Note: downloads made 18–19 Nov 2026 fall under v2.0.
- The Artlist "permitting assets in AI" PDF could not be text-extracted (compressed PDF, no pdftotext/poppler available). The exact wording relies on the CC Hound secondary summary.
- Envato's AI Product Terms and the Elements-specific AUP/Fair Use Policy were not read directly.
- The official Epidemic Sound member-licence AI clause was not retrieved. The Artlist primary licence page returned 403.
- No community (Gearspace / r/sounddesign) discussion was found interpreting Sonniss's "Usage" heading.

## 3. Bulk download and local indexing vs per-asset; API availability (incl. Freesound API commercial terms)

### Takeaway
One-time pack sources (Kenney, Sonniss GDC, 99Sounds, A Sound Effect / BOOM purchases, Adobe bundles) are made for bulk download and local indexing. Subscription and stock sites mostly forbid scraping and stockpiling:
- **Motion Array** explicitly bans "warehousing … downloading for contingent future use".
- **Pixabay** bans bulk or systematic copying without permission.
- **Zapsplat** bans scripts and bots.
- **Freesound's API** is free only for non-commercial use; commercial use is negotiated case by case with UPF.

### Cited Findings
- **Sonniss GDC**: distributed as .zip archives per year. Sizes: 2015 9.0 GB, 2016 12.9 GB, 2017 19.2 GB, 2018 29.3 GB, 2019 26.2 GB, 2020 48.2 GB, 2021–2023 38.7 GB, 2024 23.5 GB. There was no 2025 bundle; GDC 2026 has its own page. — [Sonniss GDC page](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)
- **Freesound API terms**:
  - "Terms for commercial use of the API will be negotiated on a case by case basis with UPF."
  - Call volume may be limited.
  - Apps may keep "limited intermediate copies of Content only as necessary to perform an activity permitted under these terms", must delete them when no longer needed, and "Do not make full copies of the database."
  - No "robots, spiders, scraping or other technology" beyond what Freesound provides.
  - "We may charge for this service in the future."
  - Source: [Freesound API ToS](https://freesound.org/help/tos_api/)
- **Freesound API** offers "a RESTful API through which third-party applications can access and retrieve audio content" and metadata. — [Wikipedia: Freesound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freesound)
- Project memory (verified 2026-11-18): Openverse's audio endpoint mostly returns Freesound `-hq.mp3` previews, not originals. Serving Freesound audio in a product needs legal review even for CC0. — internal note `memory/asset-sources.md`
- **Pixabay Terms (20 Oct 2026), Section 8**: prohibits "Data mining, extraction, scraping and the use of programs or robots for automatic data collection". Also: "Bulk, large-scale or systematic copying of Content is strictly prohibited unless explicit permission has been granted by us." — [Pixabay Terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)
- **Zapsplat**: "Automated access (bots, scrapers, scripts) is prohibited". Accounts may be suspended for "excessive, suspicious, or automated activity". Free users have download limits. Premium is unlimited under a Fair Use Policy. — [Zapsplat Standard licence](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/)
- **Motion Array ToS**: downloads capped at 150 per day, automated downloading forbidden, and "warehousing, aggregation, and any other downloading for contingent future use is strictly prohibited." — [Motion Array ToS (via search)](https://adobe-panel-api.motionarray.com/terms-of-service)
- **Envato**: the licence says Items used in a completed End Product during the subscription stay licensed. Otherwise "the license for the Item is terminated" when the subscription ends, so unused downloads can't sit in a library afterwards. — [Envato licence terms](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-License-Terms)
- **Artlist**: an asset downloaded while subscribed but used in a project published after cancelling is not covered. — [CC Hound](https://www.cchound.com/artlist/artlist-license/)
- **A Sound Effect**: sells perpetual per-library licences, priced per number of workstation licences. — [A Sound Effect licence](https://asoundeffect.com/license-agreement)
- **Adobe**: offers downloadable SFX bundles from about 50 MB to 1.4 GB (ambience, foley, impacts, weather and more). — [Adobe Audition SFX downloads](https://www.adobe.com/products/audition/offers/adobeauditiondlcsfx.html)
- **ElevenLabs SFX API**: model `eleven_text_to_sound_v3`. The older `eleven_text_to_sound_v2` is deprecated and will be removed 1 Mar 2027. Duration 0.1–30 s, looping supported, MP3, or WAV 48 kHz for non-looping effects. Costs 40 credits per second when duration is specified. — [ElevenLabs SFX docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/sound-effects)
- **Stable Audio Open 1.0**: downloadable open weights (runs locally), up to 47 s stereo at 44.1 kHz. Better on SFX and field recordings than music; cannot do realistic vocals. — [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-audio-open-1.0)

### Inferences
- The architecture already chosen ("owner downloads, engine indexes a curated subset locally") fits Kenney, Sonniss, 99Sounds, Adobe bundles and perpetual marketplace purchases (A Sound Effect, BOOM).
- **Zapsplat Premium can feed the local library only via manual, human-paced downloads.** Each download stays licensed for life, so a one-time curated download sprint of a few hundred gap-filling sounds works without scripts.
- **Freesound**: hand-curate CC0 sounds via the website (normal user downloads) and store licence, author and URL per file, rather than using the API in a commercial product without a UPF agreement. An agreement with UPF is the alternative.
- **Subscription stock libraries (Motion Array, Envato, Artlist, Epidemic, Storyblocks)** conflict with a persistent local library. Licences terminate for unused downloads, and some explicitly ban warehousing. They only suit per-project, at-edit-time downloads tied to an active subscription.
- **AI generation (ElevenLabs API or local Stable Audio Open)** is the only fully "on-demand via API" option that fits automated use. ElevenLabs' rule against distributing output "as isolated audio files or samples" still allows caching generated SFX internally, since they are never distributed raw.

### Gaps
- Whether Pixabay's public API serves audio/SFX (the repo currently uses it for b-roll) was not verified, and neither was whether Pixabay would grant permission for systematic SFX copying.

## 5. Pricing (paid sources) and what happens after a subscription ends

### Takeaway
Perpetual-licence sources (Zapsplat Premium downloads, Sonniss, A Sound Effect, BOOM, ElevenLabs output made on a paid plan) survive cancellation. Subscription stock libraries (Epidemic, Artlist, Envato, Motion Array) only protect work **published** during the subscription, which is a bad fit for a persistent local library. Pricing figures below are mostly from aggregators and should be confirmed on vendor sites.

### Cited Findings
- **Zapsplat Premium**: downloads made while Premium stay licensed attribution-free for life. — [Zapsplat licence](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/)
- Zapsplat price: about $15/mo for Premium (aggregator, 2026); plan names vary across sources. — [BityClips Zapsplat pricing](https://bityclips.com/tool/zapsplat/pricing)
- **Soundsnap**: licence is "revocable, perpetual (for the duration of the applicable copyright)". Post-cancellation status is not addressed. — [Soundsnap licence](https://www.soundsnap.com/licence)
- Soundsnap price: about $149/yr entry (aggregator; possibly outdated, since Soundsnap now runs a unified subscription). — [BityClips Soundsnap](https://bityclips.com/tool/soundsnap/pricing); [G2](https://g2.com/products/soundsnap/pricing)
- **Epidemic Sound**:
  - Help centre: "All content published while the subscription was active remains cleared to monetize permanently." Using music in new content after it ends "will result in monetization by Epidemic Sound". The article refers to music only, not SFX.
  - The Pro plan page: "All videos published during an active subscription will be cleared forever."
  - Price: Personal/Creator about $9.99/mo (aggregators, figures conflict).
  - Sources: [Epidemic help: subscription cancelled](https://www.epidemicsoundhelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/26254650213266-What-happens-when-a-subscription-is-canceled); [Epidemic Pro plan](https://www.epidemicsound.com/our-plans/pro-plan/); [Photutorial](https://photutorial.com/brands/epidemic-sound/)
- **Envato**:
  - Clause 8: an Item incorporated into a completed End Product during the subscription "continues for the life of the End Product, even after your subscription ends". Otherwise the Item licence is terminated. Registered uses stay covered.
  - Price: entry Core plan about $16.50/mo billed annually (aggregator).
  - Sources: [Envato licence terms](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-License-Terms); [Uppbeat comparison](https://uppbeat.io/blog/stock-media-subscription-platforms-for-editors-5-all-in-one-options-compared)
- **Artlist**: published projects stay covered. Assets downloaded while subscribed but published after cancelling are not covered, and neither are new projects using old downloads. — [CC Hound](https://www.cchound.com/artlist/artlist-license/)
- **Motion Array**: cancelled or downgraded subscribers' assets "can't be used in new or unpublished projects", and "Each new project requires an active subscription at the time it is published." — [Motion Array licence FAQ](https://help.motionarray.com/hc/en-us/articles/37251636136605-License-FAQs)
- **Storyblocks**: §1.2's perpetual right "survives the expiration, non-renewal, or termination of your subscription" but "is limited to Stock Files incorporated into Subscriber Works that are completed during a paid subscription term". — [Storyblocks Individual License](https://www.storyblocks.com/license/individual-license)
- **Storyblocks** pricing (aggregators, conflicting): about $18/mo Essentials, about $25/mo Unlimited All Access, and a $149 audio tier mentioned elsewhere. — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/storyblocks-audio/pricing); [Uppbeat comparison](https://uppbeat.io/blog/stock-media-subscription-platforms-for-editors-5-all-in-one-options-compared)
- **ElevenLabs** pricing (aggregators):
  - Free: 10k credits/mo, no commercial licence.
  - Starter: $6/mo (or $60/yr), 30k credits, commercial licence.
  - Creator: $22/mo, about 100k–121k credits.
  - Pro $99, Scale $299, Business $990 per month.
  - Output made on a paid plan reportedly keeps commercial rights after downgrade (third-party).
  - Sources: [BigVU](https://bigvu.tv/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-2026-plans-credits-commercial-rights-api-costs/); [ElevenLabs help (via search)](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/can-i-publish-the-content-i-generate-on-the-platform/llms.txt)
- **Sonniss GDC**: free, lifetime, non-expiring licence. — [Sonniss GDC page](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)
- **A Sound Effect**: perpetual, priced per workstation licence. — [A Sound Effect licence](https://asoundeffect.com/license-agreement)

### Inferences
- **ElevenLabs cost per second of SFX**: at 40 credits/s, Starter (30k credits) gives about 750 s/month, roughly 150–375 short 2–5 s SFX. Creator (~121k) gives about 3,000 s/month. That is enough to generate a few hundred custom whooshes, risers and UI sounds in a month or two. Output generated on a paid plan can be cached locally.

## 6. Content ID / copyright-claim issues with SFX

### Takeaway
YouTube's policy makes sound effects ineligible as Content ID references, so any claim on a pure SFX is invalid and disputable. Problems still happen. They are mostly false matches when a library sound appears inside a registered music track, or with mis-registered nature sounds. Subscription music/SFX vendors (Epidemic) actively monetise or claim content published after a subscription lapses. CC0 and perpetual SFX carry low claim risk, but keep proof of licence per asset to dispute claims.

### Cited Findings
- Reported false claims:
  - A music publisher (BFM Digital) reportedly claimed an indie film over a wind sound.
  - Content ID flagged ocean-wave sounds in 2012.
  - A Final Cut bundled SFX ("Thunder and Lightning 3") was reportedly claimed on behalf of a recording artist.
  - Sources: [Larry Jordan](https://larryjordan.com/articles/youtube-filmmakers-presumed-guilty-until-maybe-proven-innocent/); [Consumerist](https://consumerist.com/2012/02/27/youtube-user-i-received-copyright-violation-notice-over-bird-sounds/); [Apple discussions](https://discussions-jp-prz.apple.com/thread/255748183)
- A Freesound forum post explains that a sound embedded in a song registered with Content ID can trigger matches for other uploaders, usually resolved by dispute. — [Freesound forum](https://freesound.org/forum/legal-help-and-attribution-questions/41930)
- **YouTube's own policy**: the "Content eligible for Content ID" help page lists "Sound effects, soundbeds, or production loops" among examples not eligible for use as a reference. Third-party guides add that non-exclusively licensed royalty-free material and Creative Commons content are also excluded. — [YouTube Help: Content eligible for Content ID](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2605065); [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/music-fingerprinting-platforms-what-content-is-eligible-and-what-isnt/)
- A sound-library vendor blog makes the same argument for disputes. The search result did not make clear whether it was Zapsplat's or TunePocket's, so treat it only as corroboration. — [TunePocket](https://www.tunepocket.com/false-copyright-claims-youtube/)
- Epidemic: new content published after a subscription ends "will result in monetization by Epidemic Sound" (music). — [Epidemic help](https://www.epidemicsoundhelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/26254650213266-What-happens-when-a-subscription-is-canceled)
- Envato forbids customers from claiming ownership of Items, giving the example of applying "Content ID" or similar systems to music in your video. — [Envato licence terms](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-License-Terms)

### Inferences
- **Per-asset provenance**: the engine should keep an asset-provenance record for every SFX used in each delivered reel (source, licence version or download date, URL, file hash). That makes disputes trivial and supports audits.
- **AI-generated SFX**: ElevenLabs or Stable Audio output is very unlikely to be claimed. It may also be unprotectable by the client (ownership isn't stated in the ElevenLabs SFX terms).
- **TikTok and Instagram rights systems**: no evidence found of SFX-specific issues on either.

### Gaps
- No data on TikTok or Instagram audio-matching false positives for SFX.

### Inferences
- **Ranking criteria**: an AI-editor product needs three things at once: perpetual rights to a locally indexed file, no "AI application/usage" ban, and no attribution. Only Kenney, Freesound CC0, 99Sounds, Zapsplat Premium downloads, ElevenLabs paid output, and (pending checks) Mixkit, Adobe, A Sound Effect, BOOM and Sonniss meet all three.
- **Sonniss**:
  - It is still the highest-value free source for whooshes, impacts, booms and ambience.
  - The v2.0 "developing … or enhancing" language and "Usage" heading make a written confirmation worthwhile before relying on it commercially inside an AI product.
  - Until confirmed, index it by filename and metadata only. Don't compute learned embeddings over it, and never feed it to any model-training or fine-tuning loop.
- **Synthesis vs libraries**: the engine already synthesises 47 SFX. Risers, sweeps, whooshes and UI blips are the categories DSP or AI generation handles best. Crowd/laughs and realistic foley (cash, typing) are better sourced from Freesound CC0, Zapsplat or Sonniss recordings.
- **Provenance record**: every indexed file should carry `source`, `licence_id`, `licence_version_or_download_date`, `attribution_required`, `ai_restriction_level` and `origin_url`. The editor can then hard-filter by client/platform and dispute claims.
